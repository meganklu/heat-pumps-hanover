New England Heat Pump Accelerator

The NEHPA Community Grant

NEHPA provides funding through community grants to help low- and moderate-income households and communities adopt heat pumps. Hanover is joining a regional application, alongside three other community partners, aimed squarely at reducing the barriers renters face in accessing these benefits.

Visit the official NEHPA page →

Who's involved

Hanover is one of four community partners on this application, coordinated locally by Sustainable Hanover. The Town of Lebanon is serving as the lead organization for the group, since it already has a grant writer and the administrative staff needed to manage federal grant award requirements.

Timeline

NEHPA runs multiple Request for Applications (RFA) rounds. The closest upcoming deadline is August 14, 2026 — too soon for a fully developed application, so the partnership is aiming for the next submission round in early 2027 instead, using the time to build a stronger, better-researched proposal.

What gets evaluated

How NEHPA scores applications

Per NEHPA's published criteria, applications are assessed on three fronts:

How well the project addresses existing barriers to heat pump adoption, and whether the approach could be scaled across the wider region.

A structured, efficient budget; a clearly defined geographic area or population; and applicant qualifications, experience, and expertise.

A concrete plan to deliver benefits to low- and moderate-income households, and involvement of a variety of regional stakeholders, team members, and project beneficiaries.

Our role

What this project is contributing

The stated purpose of our section of the proposal is "reducing obstacles for renters to access the benefits of heat pump technology." We're building that section from the ground up using direct research in Hanover, rather than starting from assumptions.

  • 1

    Research & community consultation

    Surveying renters, landlords, and property managers; interviewing local officials, contractors, and past grant recipients; and analyzing Hanover rental and energy-use data to understand exactly where the barriers are.

  • 2

    Grant application writing

    Drafting the renter-barriers section of the application in parallel with ongoing research, so findings can be incorporated as they come in rather than added after the fact.

  • 3

    Preparing for implementation

    Building early frameworks for tenant and landlord outreach — including a mockup comparing a typical Hanover rental before and after a heat pump upgrade — so our community partners have a head start once funding is in place.
