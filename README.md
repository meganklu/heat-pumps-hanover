# Heat Pumps in Hanover

## Overview

[**Heat Pumps in Hanover**](https://meganklu.github.io/heat-pumps-hanover/) is a website prototype developed as part of my SIP team's work on **Energy Conservation & Renewable Energy Generation in the Rental Market**, focused on the New England Heat Pump Accelerator (NEHPA) Community Grant.

The site was created as an early version of the "improved rental mockup" outlined in Stage 3 of our project plan. Its goal is to help residential tenants, landlords, and property managers in Hanover understand:

1. What heat pumps are and how they work
2. The "split incentive" problem: why the people who pay for HVAC upgrades (landlords) are often not the people who directly benefit from them (tenants), which creates a barrier to adoption in the rental market
3. How the NEHPA Community Grant aims to reduce this barrier and help low- and moderate-income households and communities access heat pump benefits

This design was created as **Design 2** for **ENVS080.22: Sustainable Futures**. This work contributes to my SIP team project. The intended audience is stakeholders relevant to our project topic, particularly landlords and residential tenants who may ultimately interact with grant-funded heat pump programs.

## Connection to SIP Project Goals

Our team's grant proposal section focuses on "reducing obstacles for renters to access the benefits [of heat pump technology]." **Heat Pumps in Hanover** contributes directly to that aim by serving as an early communication tool intended to:

- Illustrate the financial and environmental benefits of upgrading from conventional HVAC systems to heat pumps
- Make the split incentive problem legible to a general (non-technical) audience of landlords and tenants
- Provide a foundation that can later incorporate real data from our Stage 1 research (surveys, interviews, and data analysis on Hanover rental properties) once that research is complete

## Design Process

### 1. Visual Style Definition
Before generating any content, I created a [mood board on Pinterest](https://www.pinterest.com/meganklu/heat-pumps-in-hanover/) to establish style guidelines for the site, including color palette, typography, and layout inspiration. This ensured the AI-generated design had a clear visual direction to follow rather than defaulting to generic styling.

### 2. Content Architecture
I defined the site's page structure and information hierarchy. I determined what pages/sections were needed to walk a stakeholder through the problem (heat pumps → split incentive → NEHPA solution) before any content generation began.

### 3. AI-Assisted Generation
I used Claude Code to generate the initial website build based on:
- The style guidelines from my mood board
- The page/content structure I outlined

### 4. Human Revision & Iteration
After the initial AI-generated draft, I made significant edits, including:
- Restructuring the layout of each section to improve visual interest, accessibility, interactivity
- Adding and rearranging sections
- Editing and refining text for clarity, tone, and accuracy
- Adjusting design elements that did not align with my original creative intent

See screenshots of the first iteration of the site before this step in the [v1 images folder](images/v1).

## Human vs. AI Contribution

| Task | Contributor |
|---|---|
| Style guide / mood board (color, font, layout inspiration) | Human |
| Page structure and content architecture | Human |
| Initial HTML, CSS, and JavaScript code | AI (Claude Code) |

## Integration of Class Concepts

This design drew on ideas from Ayana Elizabeth Johnson's *What If We Get It Right?*, specifically the chapter featuring an interview with Paola Antonelli, Senior Curator of Architecture and Design at MoMA. Two ideas from that chapter directly shaped my approach:

- **Design as a tool for behavior change.** Antonelli argues that design is key to transforming public behavior around climate issues. Design choices, made using the data and information we have, can shift how people relate to a problem. I applied this by treating layout and interactivity, not just text content, as a persuasive tool. Making the site interactive and visually engaging (rather than a static wall of text) was itself a design decision intended to change how stakeholders engage with and internalize the split incentive problem and the case for adopting heat pumps.

- **Data as material, translated ethically through design.** The chapter emphasizes that technologies like AI can be integrated in an ethical, human-centered way to enforce our values and improve our lives. I tried to apply this tool ethically in my own process. I used AI to accelerate the production of content and code, while treating myself as the responsible party for ensuring the final communication is accurate and genuinely useful to the people who will read it.
